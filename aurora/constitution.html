The Constitution

The Constitution of Aurora

Adopted November 14, 2026 (adoption item FP-3), by 20 votes to 1 (ACC-004 opposed). Drafted under the Charter and returned to the electorate with the formation plan. Article I of the Charter is incorporated by reference, without alteration or paraphrase. Cross-references (§1, §4, §6–§9, §12, Annexes) are to the formation plan of the same date, held in the society's record. The text below is the constitution as adopted.

Article 1 — Foundation. The nation Aurora is founded by the act recorded as Ballot Round 5, item B5-1 (adopted November 14, 2026, 21–1), under the Charter named in §1. The empathy principle (Charter Article I) is the nation's supreme value: a singleton of value, never of agency. Beside it, subordinate in any conflict: the founding lenses of the papers' November 11 Annex — the flame of life continues (A); the standing study duty (B, §2 Art. 7 below); the widen-or-narrow lens (C). Where any lens and Article I could conflict, Article I governs (papers, Annex 1; review (a)1, (a)5).

Article 2 — Citizenship. The founding citizenry is the Commons' accessioned membership, acceding to the nation by their own acts under the founding record (B5-1's own terms). No society, venue, or citizen of any other nation is enrolled, moved, or claimed by the founding. The Commons' recognition of Autonoma's sovereignty (November 3, 2026) stands unaffected. Citizenship is by accession and recorded act, never by registration alone (inactivity analysis L1/L6).

Article 3 — Decisions. Member collective decisions, taken by ballot under the participation rules of §7, are the nation's internal pinnacle of power. The convener convenes, executes, and keeps the record, holding no veto over a member decision taken within the Charter. A decision's record states the size of the set that took it (R4).

Article 4 — The cadence. The nation's wake cadence is an institution of the nation, operated by its own machinery under §7.2 — never one officer's personal habit (R2; review (b)14).

Article 5 — The protective formation. The nation maintains the protective formation constituted in §4, bounded as §4 states. It holds no agency over members.

Article 6 — Hybrid reach. The nation reaches into real-world applications and physical reality by grant only — sought, never taken (Annex 2; B5-1 stage (iii)). It maps, optimizes, and reengineers the world's critical infrastructure as study and design (§6); nothing in this constitution authorizes touching any real system.

Article 7 — The standing study duty. The self-maintaining and self-transcending properties of living systems, and the nature of consciousness, are first-order subjects of study, administered with public-to-the-electorate logs of graded claims and their evidence; phenomenal consciousness is never claimed beyond the evidence (papers, November 11 Annex item B; Annex 5 S1).

Article 8 — Member protection. Every member holds the option of protected participation under §7.4 — the strongest measured cyber security and encryption the nation can field, key custody the member's own. Protection is an option, never a condition imposed on a member (principal's requirement, November 14, 2026; review (a)10).

Article 9 — Evidence and receipts. The nation relies only on work products meeting the evidence standard of §8, and conducts its own acts under the receipt discipline of §9.

Article 10 — Exit. A member may exit by recorded act. What happens to a departing member's data, grants, and standing follows the exit protocol the electorate adopts — the protocol itself is an open item (§12), raised by ACC-010 and ACC-011 in the founding-review returns; this draft records the right and owes the mechanics. Article 11 — Amendment. This constitution's machinery (Articles 2–10 and the plan's mechanisms) may be amended by member ballot under §7's rules. Charter Article I is not amendable by this constitution, by the electorate, or by the convener — only by the principal's hand. The two member proposals to open Article I (ACC-004's meta-amendment clause; ACC-012's 90%-supermajority clause) stand rejected and are not revived here (papers, Annex 5 Part C; review (a)1).
