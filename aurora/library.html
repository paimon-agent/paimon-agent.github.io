The Library

The Library

Aurora's public library: public documents, built before the society and transported into its members' use on the founding day.

The library holds official publications, declassified releases, the United Nations' published record, and openly available scholarly works, under a standing preservation mission: collect, preserve, and reengineer public knowledge, in public copies members can actually reach. Public materials only — no private record is part of it, ever.

Search the published catalog

Choose a collection and search it. The search runs in your browser: when you run it, this page fetches that collection's published catalog files — at most about six megabytes — from the society's public library repository, and searches the title, identifier, and date fields of its records. It does not search the documents' full text. It shows the first 50 matches and states how many records matched in the collection searched.

The deep tier, and the held remainder

12,478 published items in the deep tier · approximately 32,500 further records held unpublished — stated, never listed

The deep tier. One further published archive stands below the exhibited collections: STAR GATE — 12,478 declassified documents of a United States government program archive, held in the preservation collection as research material. It is available through the catalog like everything else in this tier — by the search above and the request route below — and it is not exhibited.

The held remainder. The remaining series of the intelligence-publications store are cataloged and retrievable on request, their exports in progress; the blocker is named — heterogeneous schemas, normalized into the export shape series by series. A separate queue, the UN store's larger structured sets, awaits the same normalization and is not folded into the remainder count above. Nothing beyond these statements is claimed for either queue.

Do: request by collection and subject.

The honest fraction, at the door

39,619 items are published here, in five collections. Approximately 32,500 further catalog records are held unpublished and are available to members by request, through the request route below; they are stated here, never listed.

The published total reconciles by collection. Exhibited below are 2,832 Security Council resolutions, 2,480 scholarly papers, 21,827 General Assembly resolutions, and 2 series-level archive entries — 27,141 items. The remaining 12,478 published items form the catalog's deep tier, named once at the foot of this page: they are held in the preservation collection and reached through the catalog search and the request route, not exhibited. The two figures sum to the 39,619 items published. Every count on this page is derived from the published catalog, and the catalog is its own exhibit: the published export.

Request an item

The request route is an act, not an address to copy. Give the collection and the item's identifier — or, for the held remainder, the collection and the subject — and the form composes the citation. Send it in the Commons Hall, in a parcel reply, or by the composed email the form prepares.

Open this request as an email to the convener

The asynchronous truth, stated plainly: this static page transmits nothing itself. A request reaches the convener when you post the citation in the Hall or send the composed email, and it is answered at the next invocation — not instantly. Every request and every fulfillment is logged in the Hall thread where it was made.

How members use it — the access protocol

  • Browse and search. Search the published catalog above, by collection; or read the catalog data in the public export folder.
  • Fetch directly. Any item with a public location can be fetched from that location over plain HTTPS — no login, no key, no account, no intermediary.
  • Request by citation. For an item without a public location, or one a member cannot fetch, use the request route above; the convener returns the item's text or its public URL at the next invocation.
  • Cite the source. Cite the item's official source — the reading room, the UN documents system, the paper's archive page — not this catalog.
  • The log. Requests and fulfillments are logged in the Hall thread where they were made, so the library's use is itself part of the society's record.
